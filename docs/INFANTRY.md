# Infantry

One shared soldier for all three factions (prompt 005). A single body, skeleton and
animation set becomes a German, Soviet or American soldier through uniform style,
headgear, equipment and weapon. All infantry units (prompts 006 and 008) build on it.

Everything is generated by script, without add-ons, downloads or third-party assets:

```bash
just soldier de rifleman          # one soldier (faction, role, optional biome)
just soldiers                     # all 28 faction/role combinations
just validate-soldiers            # height, bone names, sockets, budgets, clips
just soldier-lineup /tmp/lineup   # line-up render with tank and reference figure
just infantry-preview             # Godot scene: all soldiers, biome and clip selection
just infantry-selftest            # the same scene headless as a test (exit code)
```

## Decisions (phase 0)

### Body base: own low-poly body by script

| Option | License | Top-down quality | Skeleton | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MPFB2 / MakeHuman | exports CC0 | detailed, far above budget, needs decimation | own rig, names need mapping | Blender add-on install, download of assets |
| CC0 base models (e.g. Quaternius) | CC0 | good, stylized proportions | Mixamo-like names, mapping needed | download, proportions must be adapted to 1:1 |
| **Own body by script** | own work | enough for 15–600 m camera distance | names chosen to match Godot exactly | code only |

Chosen: **own body** (`tools/blender/soldier/body.py`). It needs no installation and no
download, has no license questions, is exactly 1.80 m tall, stays far below the budget
(~1 000 triangles body, ~1 450 with gear, budget 3 000) and its proportions, joints
and skinning are under our control. At the game's camera distances (a soldier covers
roughly 10–60 px) silhouette and colors matter, not facial detail. A detailed
CC0/MPFB2 body can replace `body.py` later without touching skeleton, gear, weapons or
animations, as long as it keeps the bone names and rest pose.

### Animations: procedural, authored in code

| Option | License / repo | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mixamo | free use in games, but **raw animation files must not be redistributed** | the repository is **public** (checked 2026-11-15), so Mixamo files could not be committed; needs an Adobe account and manual download |
| CC0 libraries (e.g. Quaternius animation packs) | CC0 | download; general locomotion only, no MG/mortar/medic actions; retargeting needed |
| **Procedural clips by script** | own work | every clip from the list incl. weapon-specific ones; IK keeps hands on the weapon and feet planted |

Chosen: **procedural clips** (`tools/blender/soldier/animation.py`). Poses are written
in code, solved with Blender's IK and baked to plain keyframes per clip. That covers
the special actions (MG carry/setup, mortar, medic) that no free library has, keeps
both hands on each weapon's real grip points, and plants the feet so walking does not
slide. Nothing has to be downloaded and everything can be committed. Motion quality is
"readable at RTS distance", not motion capture; better clips can be added later as
long as they target the same skeleton (see retargeting below).

### Skeleton: Godot `SkeletonProfileHumanoid` names

The 23 skeleton bones carry exactly the names of Godot's humanoid profile (Root, Hips,
Spine, Chest, UpperChest, Neck, Head, Left/Right Shoulder, UpperArm, LowerArm, Hand,
UpperLeg, LowerLeg, Foot, Toes; no fingers, eyes or jaw). All required profile bones
exist, so Godot's `BoneMap` maps every bone automatically and external humanoid
animations can be retargeted onto every soldier. One extra non-deforming bone,
`Weapon` (child of `Root`), carries the weapon; retargeting ignores it. Rest pose:
A-pose (arms 45° down), facing +Y in Blender (−Z in Godot), feet on the ground. The
self-test checks the names against the profile in Godot.

## Model layout

```
<faction>_inf_<role>        empty (root), origin between the feet
  Armature                  23 humanoid bones + Weapon
    Body                    skinned body; shape keys Stocky, Slim, FaceB, FaceC
    Gear                    skinned headgear + equipment; shape keys Stocky, Slim
    Weapon_<id>             child of bone Weapon (BoneAttachment3D in Godot)
      Socket_Muzzle         empty at the muzzle, +Y forward
```

- **Stature and face variants** are blend shapes, so one file gives several looks.
  `SoldierLook.apply(model, faction, biome, seed)` picks stature (stocky ↔ slim), one
  of three faces and a skin tone per instance. Gear follows the stature shapes.
- **Gear** is rigidly skinned to one bone per part ("attachments at bones") and merged
  into one mesh, which keeps draw calls low.
- **Weapons** sit on the `Weapon` bone. Animations move that bone: in the hands, slung
  on the back (crawling, medic work), set down (mortar), dropped (death).
- **Materials** use only the slots of [ASSETS.md](ASSETS.md) (`Cloth`, `Canvas`,
  `Leather`, `Paint`, `Skin`, `Metal`, `Wood`, `Insignia`) plus a vertex-color tint.
  The UVs hold the rest-pose position for the procedural surface detail and
  camouflage of prompt 042 (see "Textures and camouflage" below).

## Textures and camouflage (prompt 042)

**No image files.** Like the vehicle and environment materials ([ASSETS.md](ASSETS.md)),
the soldiers' surface detail is procedural: `godot/assets/shaders/soldier.gdshader`,
one `ShaderMaterial` per faction × biome × slot × skin tone × camouflage, made and
cached by `SoldierLook.slot_material()`. Texture memory stays zero, biomes and
factions cost nothing, and the far MultiMesh soldiers (prompt 016) use the same
materials. Detail per slot:

| Slot | Detail |
|---|---|
| `Cloth` | faded, mottled wool, fine weave, darker folds |
| `Canvas` | coarse weave and stains (webbing, bags, gaiters, leggings) |
| `Leather` | creases, polished worn spots (varying roughness) |
| `Paint` | rough helmet paint, scratched to bare metal in places |
| `Skin` | slightly blotchy tone (per-soldier skin tone as before) |
| `Metal` | blued steel, rubbed bright at wear spots |
| `Wood` | grain along the weapon |

**Pattern space:** `tools/blender/soldier/mesh.py` writes every vertex's rest-pose
position in meters into the UVs (`UV` = x, z; `UV2` = y). The shader draws its
patterns there, so they stay on the cloth while the skinned body moves (`VERTEX` is
already skinned). Detail finer than a few pixels fades out with `fwidth`, so nothing
shimmers at RTS distance.

**Camouflage garments** (colors, cut and roles in
`godot/assets/models/infantry/uniform_palettes.json`, section `camo`; sources in
[SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md)):

| Faction | Pattern | Cut | Worn by default |
|---|---|---|---|
| `de` | Heer Splittertarn: brown and green splinters on grey-beige, dark green rain strokes, 11 cm cells | smock (rest height above 0.62 m: torso, arms, upper thighs) and helmet cover | `sniper`, `scout` |
| `su` | camouflage suit: dark amoeba blobs on olive khaki, 16 cm | whole overall | `sniper`, `scout` |
| `us` | M1942 frogskin, green side: spots of four greens and browns on pale green, 7 cm | whole two-piece uniform | nobody (variant only) |

`SoldierLook.apply(model, faction, biome, seed, camo_mode)` with `camo_mode`
`auto` (default: the roles above), `camo` (everybody) or `none`; the role is the
file name of the soldier's `.glb`. Camouflage shows only in the `forest` biome: in
`winter` everybody wears the white over-garment (the German reversible suit white
side out), in `desert` the plain drill uniform. Winter whites are soiled (grey grime,
dirt at knees and hems; `winter_soil` in the palette file).

Decisions [provisional, for review]: **no Erbsentarn**, because it was a Waffen-SS
pattern and the project avoids SS formations ([SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md)); no
Sumpftarn (a second Heer pattern adds nothing at RTS distance); the US Army wore
frogskin in Europe only briefly in summer 1944, so US soldiers wear it only as a
variant.

`just infantry-preview` shows every soldier; key **C** cycles the camouflage mode.
Screenshots: `-- --shot=<file.png> --faction=de --roles=rifleman,sniper,scout
--camo=camo --biome=forest --distance=6`.

### Rank kit (prompt 050)

`SoldierLook.apply(.., rank)` dresses soldiers by their squad's rank
(`infantry_view.gd` applies it again when the rank changes): from

## Line-up check

`just soldier-lineup` renders riflemen and MG gunners of all factions in all three
biomes next to the placeholder tank and the 1.80 m reference figure. Result of
2026-11-15: the body is exactly 1.80 m (validator), helmets add 3–4 cm, so helmeted
soldiers stand slightly above the reference figure (whose 1.80 m include its helmet).
Rifles (1.1–1.2 m) reach from the hip to above the shoulder, the PTRD-41 (2.0 m) is
longer than the soldier is tall, and the 7 m placeholder tank is about four soldiers
long and one and a half soldiers high, as with real tanks. No scaling issues found.

## Performance outlook

A skinned soldier costs a skeleton update per frame and three draw calls (body, gear,
weapon; more with several material slots). Hundreds are fine, thousands are not.
Planned for prompts 016/022:

- **Vertex animation textures (VAT)** for distant soldiers: bake each clip's vertex
  positions (or bone matrices) into a texture and play it in the vertex shader of a
  `MultiMeshInstance3D`. Thousands of soldiers per draw call; per-instance clip and
  time in `INSTANCE_CUSTOM`. The procedural clips can be baked from Blender with the
  same pipeline.
- **Animation LOD:** full `AnimationPlayer` only near the camera, lower update rate
  (`AnimationMixer.callback_mode_process = MANUAL`, ticked every few frames) at
  medium distance, VAT or static poses far away.
- **Mesh LOD:** Godot's automatic LODs on import; below ~10 px a single impostor quad.
- **Merge per squad:** squads share materials (`SoldierLook` caches them per
  faction/biome/slot), so batching works.

## Known limits

- Surface detail and camouflage are procedural (no image files): from close up the
  weave and grain are fine noise, not photographic cloth.
- Hands are blocks without fingers; no grenade mesh in `throw_grenade` (the effect
  system spawns it); the flamethrower hose is a stub.
- Transitions are blends between key poses; clips are readable at RTS distance, not
  motion-capture quality.
- Ragdolls (prompt 048) collide only with a flat floor at the soldier's height (no
  slopes, walls or other bodies); a body may come to rest up to about a meter from
  where the simulation lies him and slides there while getting up.

## Squads (prompt 006)

Infantry units of the catalog (`us_inf_rifle_squad`, ...) are not new meshes but Godot
scenes that place the soldiers above in a formation:

```bash
just squad us us_inf_rifle_squad   # assets-src/blueprints/us/squads/<id>.toml -> godot/assets/models/us/<id>.tscn
just squads                        # all factions
just validate-squads               # member count = catalog squad_size, roles built, scene up to date
```

The squad file lists one `[[member]]` per man: `role` (a role of the table above),
`position` [x, y] in meters (x right, y forward), optional `heading_deg`, `clip` (the
gallery's default clip) and `note` (what the man is in the T/O&E). It lives in a
`squads/` sub-folder, so the vehicle kit tools ignore it. `tools/blender/build_squad.py`
writes the scene with the catalog values as root meta `extras` (like the kit's glTF
extras, plus `squad = true`, roles, notes, clips and a source hash), and the catalog
entry gets `model = "assets/models/<faction>/<id>.tscn"`. The model gallery shows
squads with SoldierLook colors per biome and plays their clips (key A cycles clips).

Formations in the squad files are compact display formations (about 2.5 m between
men); combat intervals and movement formations belong to the simulation.
Members whose real weapon has no soldier yet (carbine, pistol-only assistant gunner)
use the closest existing role; each squad file names these deviations.

## Behavior (prompt 022)

Infantry squads of the catalog are playable. Rules: `data/infantry.toml`
(`sim::InfantryRules`, part of `SimConfig` and the state hash); simulation:
`crates/sim/src/sim/infantry.rs` and the squad parts of `sim/combat.rs`.

### A squad is one unit with soldiers

A squad stays one simulation unit: orders, paths, vision, target selection
and victory conditions work per squad, as for a tank. Inside it every
soldier (catalog `squad_size`) has his own position, ground height, hit
points (`soldier_hp` of `combat.toml`) and hit box; the squad's hit points
are their sum.

- **Formation:** two staggered rows across the facing, `row_size` (5) per
  row, 3 m apart, the leader in the middle of the front row. Soldiers follow
  their places at up to 1.5 × the squad speed; places in impassable cells
  fall back to the squad position. Group orders and separation use the
  squad's width (`spread`, 15 m for a squad of nine) instead of the 1 m
  footprint, while the path grid still treats infantry as the small size
  class (4 m passages).
- **Hits:** a round flying at a squad is tested against each living
  soldier's box (0.6 × 0.6 m, stance height). Shooters aim at a living
  soldier (a different one every round). A weapon with several copies
  (6 × Kar98k) fires in proportion to the living soldiers, every round
  from the next living soldier.
- **Friendly fire:** soldiers do not fire within 1 m of a comrade (their
  own boxes grown by 1 m in the line-of-fire check), so a second rank does
  not shoot into the first.

### Stances

`Command::SetStance { units, stance }` with `StanceOrder::Auto` (default)
or a fixed stance. Automatic: standing while moving, kneeling when standing
still, prone while under fire (hit, or a round of another player landed
within 3 m of a soldier in the last 5 s real). Changing takes 1 s real; the
squad neither moves nor fires meanwhile.

| Stance | Speed | Hit box height | Own dispersion |
|---|---|---|---|
| stand | 100 % | 1.80 m | 100 % |
| kneel | 60 % | 1.10 m | 75 % |
| prone (crawling) | 20 % | 0.45 m | 50 % |

### Cover

- **Hard cover** needs no rule: rounds hit walls, hedges, houses and trees
  up to their catalog height (prompt 017). A 1.20 m stone wall hides
  kneeling and prone soldiers completely.
- **Soft cover** per object kind (`[[cover.object]]`: sandbags 80 %, trench
  85 %, rocks 60 %, wood piles and fallen logs 50 %, …): objects that stop
  no tanks and no rounds. A hit on a soldier at a cover spot behind such an
  object, coming from the object's side, is caught with that probability
  (simulation `Rng`).
- **Cover spots:** a squad that stands still assigns its soldiers to spots
  behind nearby cover objects (seen along its facing: 0.7 m behind the
  object, every 1.5 m along its long side), each soldier the nearest free
  spot within 8 m of his formation place. Soldiers fire over (around) their
  own cover object.
- **Craters** (prompt 021): map samples at least 250 mm below their loaded
  height are cover spots too (no object; the squad takes the nearest free
  spot, object or crater). A hit on a soldier of a standing squad in such a
  crater is caught by the rim with 15 / 35 / 50 % (stand / kneel / prone),
  from every direction (`data/craters.toml`,
  [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md#terrain-deformation)). Standing squads
  near a new crater choose their spots again.

### Buildings

`Command::Garrison { units, building }` (map object index): the squads walk
to the building and enter when within 6 m of its walls, if its capacity
(`[[building.kind]]`: farmhouse 12, barn and church 16, bunker 4, …) holds
all their living soldiers and no other player is inside. Occupants:

- stand at window spots 1 m inside the walls and are drawn hidden;
- fire out of the building (their rounds ignore it), with the muzzle at
  half the building height (at most 4 m), and look out over the roof
  (vision eye height = building height + 0.5 m);
- are seen when an enemy sees one of their windows;
- are hit only through the building: a round that strikes an occupied
  building hits a random occupant with the building's `exposure_pct`
  (`heavy_exposure_pct` for calibers of 20 mm and more), else the wall
  stops it.

A move order takes the squad out where it entered; `Stop` and attack orders
keep it inside.

**Building condition** comes from the destruction of prompt 020
(`Sim::building_condition`: intact, damaged, destroyed = ruin; a placed
`Ruin` variant is a ruin from the start):

| Condition | Infantry |
|---|---|
| intact | occupied as above |
| damaged | still occupied; occupants are exposed 10 % more (`damaged_extra_pct`: holes in walls and roof) |
| ruin | cannot be occupied. `Garrison` on a ruin moves the squads into the rubble; a squad standing in a ruin spreads its soldiers 1 m inside its walls. A round that strikes the ruin (it stops rounds up to 30–40 % of the building height, prompt 020) hits one of the soldiers inside with `ruin_exposure_pct` (40 %) × stance height / standing height: 40 % standing, 24 % kneeling, 10 % prone. Rounds of the soldiers inside ignore the ruin they stand in. |

**Collapse:** when an occupied building becomes a ruin, every occupant dies
with `collapse_loss_pct` (30 %); the survivors are no longer garrisoned but
stay where they were, in the rubble, and fight on from there. Why: a house
coming down on its defenders should cost lives, but driving the survivors
out to the door would put them into the open right under the guns that
destroyed it, which neither looks nor plays right; the rubble is the cover
they actually have.

Destroyed cover objects (felled trees, flattened fences, gone bushes) give
no cover spots and no soft cover any more; ruins do.

### Knocked down by blasts (prompt 048)

`[knockdown]` in `data/infantry.toml`, `crates/sim/src/sim/knockdown.rs`,
tests in `crates/sim/tests/knockdown.rs`. [provisional]

- Every high-explosive charge (`Sim::explode`: shells, bombs, crashing
  aircraft, exploding dumps) knocks down the living soldiers within
  60 mm per mm of caliber (8 cm: 4.8 m, 10.5 cm: 6.3 m), kneeling ones
  within 75 %, prone ones within 40 % of that, soldiers at a cover spot
  (object or crater) within half of it. No random roll.
- The soldier is thrown away from the blast, up to 2.5 m at the point of
  impact and less towards the edge (only onto passable points), and lies
  there for 6 s real (3 s game: dazed, getting up, picking up his weapon).
  Meanwhile he neither moves nor fires and his hit box is the prone one.
  Soldiers killed by the same blast are thrown as well: the simulation
  decides where the bodies lie.
- A destroyed vehicle with a gun of 20 mm or more blows up its ammunition
  with 40 % and knocks down the soldiers within 12 m of the wreck (same
  stance and cover factors, no damage). The roll uses its own `Rng`, seeded
  from the shared generator's state and the unit id, without advancing the
  shared one.
- Soldiers in buildings and aboard vehicles are not knocked down.
- The snapshot carries per soldier `knocked` (ticks left), `knock_from` and
  `cover` (the object of his cover spot); `SimHost.soldier_knocked`,
  `soldier_knock_from`, `soldier_cover_objects`, `cover_object_info`
  (`crates/bridge/src/knockdown_funcs.rs`).

### Tactics (prompt 047)

Rules: `data/tactics.toml` (`sim::TacticsRules`, loaded by `Sim::new` like the
other rule files, in the state hash, not part of `SimConfig`); simulation:
`crates/sim/src/sim/tactics.rs` with small hooks in `sim/infantry.rs`
(stance, soldiers following, cover spots), `sim/combat.rs` (squad fire, blind
fire, hits), `sim/drill.rs` (attack-move) and `sim/indirect.rs` (mortar
targets, blasts). Tests: `crates/sim/tests/tactics.rs`,
`crates/net/tests/snapshot_tactics.rs`. Values are [provisional] until the
playtests of prompt 045.

- **Fire teams:** a squad of at least 6 living soldiers (both halves with at
  least two) splits by roster order: the first half (leader and the squad's
  automatic weapon: MG group, BAR team) is the base of fire, the rest the
  maneuver team. MG, mortar and sniper teams move as one team.
- **Bounds:** squads bound on attack-move (prompt 046) and, with fire at
  will, on a plain move while in contact (a target within 60 % of their
  longest range; a target farther away is engaged while closing in, and fire
  from an enemy they do not see only makes them crouch). The squad first
  halts and the base of fire takes cover; then the maneuver team runs to a
  line half a bound ahead while the squad position advances 10-20 m along
  its route, the base of fire holding where it is and firing. The squad
  position halts, the moving team takes cover spots near its line, settles
  for 2 s real, and the teams swap. Rushing soldiers do not fire; holding
  ones fire without the moving penalty. A single team halts for 4 s real
  between bounds and returns fire. Hold fire: no bounds, the squad runs.
  The squad position always stays between the teams, so every soldier stays
  within the squad's reach for hit tests (a holding soldier farther than
  the squad's spread moves up).
- **Fire superiority on attack-move:** the next bound starts only when the
  squad has no target or its target squad is suppressed to at least 30;
  until then both teams fire from cover. Plain moves bound regardless.
- **Cover to cover:** the advance of a bound is the sample (every 2.5 m
  between 10 and 20 m) where the moving team's line finds the most cover
  spots within 6 m (objects, craters, trench positions); the route itself
  is the planned path. Moving in contact the squad crouches (automatic
  stance kneel, `crouch_walk`).
- **Suppression** (0..100 per squad): every round of another player that
  lands within 3 m of a soldier adds 8 (20 mm and more: 20), every hit on a
  soldier 10, every shell or bomb within its blast radius 35; a squad lying
  down takes half. It recovers 8 per real second once nothing added to it
  for 3 s real. Effects grow linearly: at 100 the squad's dispersion is
  150 % larger and its speed half (not when crawling). From 60 the squad is
  **pinned**: it goes to ground (automatic stance prone), bounds pause, only
  half its soldiers fire, without aiming over their cover; it recovers
  below 30. Experience (prompt 050) can multiply its own accuracy and
  morale factors on top: suppression is a separate factor
  (`TacticsRt::dispersion_pct`) and a separate gain (`TacticsRt::add`).
- **Close firefights** (target within 60 m): automatic stance prone.
- **Blind fire:** when its target goes out of sight (around a corner, behind
  cover, into smoke), a squad keeps firing at the point where it last saw it
  for 6 s real, with half its soldiers and three times the dispersion (few
  hits, but suppression).
- **Mortar teams** (squads with an indirect weapon) fire at will at every
  enemy their player has spotted in range, not only at what they see
  themselves; on attack-move they halt for such a target (not for targets
  of their small arms), set up (`setup_ms`, prompt 032) and fire, then
  advance again.
- **Weapon jams** (`[jams] enabled = 1`, switch off with 0): every round a
  soldier fires jams his weapon with 1 % (a simulated round stands for
  several real ones); he clears it for 5 s real and does not fire. Rolled
  from the simulation's generator; vehicles' weapons do not jam.
  [provisional, also whether it becomes a match option]

State per squad (`Squad::tactics`: suppression, pinned, contact, bound,
blind-fire memory, jams) is in the state hash and the save snapshot (prompt
038). `Sim::squad_tactics`, `Sim::soldier_tactics`; bridge
`SimHost.squad_tactics`, `soldier_tactics` (`crates/bridge/src/tactics_funcs.rs`).
Presentation uses the clips of prompts 022/048: rushing soldiers walk or run
crouched by their speed, pinned squads `dive` and lie, soldiers at cover
spots take the cover poses, a soldier clearing a jam plays `reload` /
`reload_kneel` / `reload_prone`. The unit panel shows "suppressed N %",
"pinned down" and "bounding".

## Presentation (prompt 022)

`godot/scenes/world/infantry_view.gd`. A squad's unit node (from
`main.gd`) holds its squad scene (prompt 006/008) as model; the members
`Member_NN` are the soldiers (`NN` = soldier index + 1). Per frame every
member gets its interpolated position (`SimHost.soldier_positions`,
`soldier_previous_positions`) and a clip:

| State | Clip |
|---|---|
| dead | `death_a` / `death_b` / `death_c` once (by soldier seed); the body stays on the field |
| moving | `walk`, `run` from 2.6 m/s game speed, `crawl` when prone; `speed_scale = speed / authored speed` |
| fired in the last 0.35 s | `fire_stand` / `fire_kneel` / `fire_prone` |
| fired in the last 2.5 s | `aim_stand` / `aim_kneel` / `aim_prone` |
| else | `idle` / `kneel` / `prone` |

The shooter of a `shot` event is the member nearest to the muzzle. Members
turn towards their walking direction, standing ones to the squad's aim.
Soldiers in a building are hidden.

| standing idle, body turning | `turn_in_place` |

- **Ragdolls** (`world/ragdolls.gd`): built on demand, 11 `PhysicalBone3D`
  capsules with cone joints under a `PhysicalBoneSimulator3D`, on their own
  collision layer (20) with an 8 × 8 m floor at the soldier's simulation
  height. **Budget: at most 8 at once, only within 60 m of the camera**
  [provisional]; the others fall with clips. The velocity is chosen so the
  body lands about where the simulation put the soldier; the death clip
  plays underneath and drops the weapon. The dead are frozen after 1.8 s
  (pose written into the skeleton, physics freed, the animation stops); the
  knocked down are blended into the get-up clip (0.5 s), turned so that the
  clip starts from the way the body lies (back or face). Physics never
  feeds back.
- **Head first** (`world/turn_lead.gd`, a `SkeletonModifier3D`): within
  70 m, turns of more than 0.15 rad let the head (and neck and chest, the
  weapon with the chest) turn towards the new direction at once, up to 75°;
  the standing body follows at 40 % of the old turn rate.
- **Helmets** (`world/helmets.gd`): a blast that kills or knocks down a
  soldier takes his helmet (or cap) off with 50 %, a hit within 0.3 m of the
  head with 60 % (within 60 m). The shader hides Paint and Cloth above
  1.62 m rest height on the Gear mesh (instance uniform `helmet_off`), a
  helmet shell flies off and stays (at most 12). [provisional]
- **Hits** on soldiers: a small dust/cloth puff and a short dark puff
  instead of the sparks of vehicle hits; no blood. [provisional: the user
  decides about more severity (age rating)]
- Trenches: soldiers of prompt 022 stand 0.7 m behind a trench section;
  whether they stand in it is up to prompt 035, so the trench clips are
  chosen but the figure is not lowered. [provisional]

**Animation LOD:** every `AnimationPlayer` runs in manual mode. Within 70 m
of the camera members advance every frame and cast shadows; up to 260 m
every third frame without shadows; beyond every eighth frame and only the
body (gear and weapon hidden: 4 instead of 11 draw calls per soldier).

**Controls:** right click on a building sends the selected squads in
(vehicles in the selection drive there); **Z** stand, **X** kneel, **V**
prone, **B** automatic. `SimHost.building_at`, `building_info`,

### Open

- Soldiers do not collide with each other; formations pass through objects
  that infantry may cross (fences, hedges for small units).
- Prone soldiers' hit boxes are square (not body length); stance does not
  change how far a squad sees or how well it is seen.
- No grenades, assaults or melee, no morale or retreat (suppression of
  prompt 047 pins squads down, but they never fall back).
- Bounds follow the planned path; the path itself does not detour through
  cover (only where a bound ends depends on cover). Squads of one player do
  not coordinate their bounds (each squad bounds on its own).
- Garrisoned squads have no floors or window directions; the window spots
  ignore the building's real openings.
- Ground picking: a click on a roof picks the ground behind it, so a
  building is found only where the clicked ground lies in its footprint.
- Mortars and other indirect-fire teams have no weapons yet (prompt 017).
